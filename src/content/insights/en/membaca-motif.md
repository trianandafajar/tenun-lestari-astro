Every weaving technique leaves a signature you can learn to recognise. Here are the four you will meet most often in our collection.

## Ikat

The motif is made by tying off sections of yarn before dyeing, so the tied parts resist the colour. The edges of an ikat motif tend to look slightly blurred, and that is exactly its charm.

## Lurik

Lurik means stripe. The pattern comes from the arrangement of coloured threads in warp and weft. Simple, yet the combinations of stripe widths are nearly endless.

## Songket

An extra weft, often gold or silver, is floated over the ground weave to form raised motifs such as stars or flowers.

## Anyaman

Warp and weft threads cross in pairs to form a check pattern like a woven mat. The weave is dense and strong, ideal for home textiles.
